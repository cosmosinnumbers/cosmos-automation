Which bright dots are planets

  • Mercury: before dawn, low in the south-east before sunrise; by the end of the period: hidden in the Sun's glare (magnitude -0.1, in Virgo)
  • Venus: before dawn, midway up in the south-east before sunrise (magnitude -4.4, in Virgo)
  • Mars: before dawn, high in the south before sunrise; by the end of the period: before dawn, high in the south-west before sunrise (magnitude 0.7, in Leo)
  • Jupiter: before dawn, high in the south before sunrise; by the end of the period: before dawn, high in the south-west before sunrise (magnitude -2.0, in Leo)
  • Saturn: evening, midway up in the south-east after sunset (magnitude 0.6, in Cetus)

Tip: a planet shines with a steady light, a star twinkles.

Dates to circle

🌙 Tue 17 NovFirst quarter
First quarter Moon: half lit in the evening, the best phase to see craters with binoculars.
☄️ Tue 17 NovLeonids meteor shower peak
Leonids peak: up to 15 meteors an hour under a perfect dark sky, debris of Comet Tempel-Tuttle. Moon 55% lit: some moonlight. Best after midnight.
🌙 Tue 24 NovFull Moon (supermoon)
Full Moon, 360,800 km away. A supermoon: closer than usual, so slightly bigger and brighter.
✨ Mon 30 NovMoon near Jupiter
The Moon (60% lit) passes about 1° from Jupiter: the easiest way to find the planet.
✨ Mon 30 NovMoon near Mars
The Moon (56% lit) passes about 3° from Mars: the easiest way to find the planet.
🌙 Tue 1 DecLast quarter
Last quarter Moon: rises around midnight, evenings stay dark.
🌙 Wed 9 DecNew Moon
New Moon: the darkest nights of the month, best for faint stars and the Milky Way.
