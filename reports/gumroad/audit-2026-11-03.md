You don't need a telescope. You need to know where to look.
Most people who say "I can't see anything up there" never learned where and how to look. LOOK UP is a practical 173-page guide that takes complete beginners from their first night outside to finding another galaxy with binoculars, with every number linked to its source.

What you'll be able to do

- Find the planets, the brightest stars and the main constellations on any clear night

- Plan the best nights using the Moon phase, light pollution (Bortle scale) and the weather

- Catch the best meteor showers of 2027 when the Moon won't spoil them

- Watch the total solar eclipse of 2 August 2027 (up to 6 min 23 s of totality) safely

- Photograph the Moon and the stars with the phone you already have

- Choose your first binoculars without wasting money

What's inside

- 10 chapters, from your first night out to phone astrophotography

- More than 30 field exercises you do outside, each with the time, kit, steps and the result you should expect

- 36 practical tables you can read by red light, plus diagrams

- A month-by-month 2027 sky calendar computed from NASA/JPL ephemeris data: Moon phases, the oppositions of Jupiter and Mars, Mercury's best dates for each hemisphere, and meteor showers ranked with the Moon taken into account

- The 30-Night Challenge: a checklist that takes you from beginner to confident observer

- A printable observing log

Who it's for
Curious beginners, parents who want a clear-sky activity with their kids, and anyone who has just bought (or is about to buy) binoculars or a first telescope. If you already run a 10-inch Dobsonian, this book is too basic for you.
FAQ

I live in a city. Will it work? Yes. The Moon, the planets and the bright stars show through light pollution, and the book tells you what to expect at each level of the Bortle scale.
Southern Hemisphere? Yes. The book covers both hemispheres where the sky differs.
